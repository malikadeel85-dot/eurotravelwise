Paris Travel Guide 2026: History, Itinerary, Costs, Hotels & Travel Tips

Plan a memorable Paris trip with this complete guide to history, famous sights, neighbourhoods, food, transport, costs, itinerary ideas, day trips and practical travel advice.

Paris is a city where history, art, architecture, food and everyday life meet along the Seine. For a first visit, four to five days is a practical target for combining major landmarks with neighbourhood walks and relaxed meals.

Related guide: See our Best Places to Visit in Paris for a focused attractions guide.

1. Introduction to Paris

Paris is the capital of France and one of Europe's major cultural and historic destinations. The Seine divides the city into the Right Bank and Left Bank, while its arrondissements give different parts of the city their own character.

A first visit normally includes the Eiffel Tower, Louvre, Notre-Dame, Arc de Triomphe, Champs-Élysées, Montmartre, Sacré-Cœur and the Seine. Paris is also about cafés, markets, bakeries, parks and simply walking through historic streets.

2. Complete History of Paris

Ancient Paris

The history of Paris goes back more than two thousand years. The Parisii, a Gallic people, lived around the Seine before Roman rule. The Romans developed the settlement known as Lutetia on and around the Île de la Cité and Left Bank.

Medieval Paris

During the Middle Ages, Paris became an important political, religious and intellectual centre. Notre-Dame began construction in the 12th century, and the medieval city developed around the Seine. The medieval Louvre began as a fortress before becoming a royal palace.

Renaissance and royal Paris

From the Renaissance onward, Paris expanded through royal building projects and new architectural styles. The Louvre evolved from fortress to palace, while Paris grew in political and cultural importance.

Enlightenment and Revolution

The 18th century brought major intellectual and political changes. Paris was central to the French Revolution beginning in 1789, an event that permanently changed France's political history.

Haussmann's 19th-century transformation

Under Napoleon III, Baron Georges-Eugène Haussmann oversaw major urban works, including broad avenues, improved infrastructure and redesigned neighbourhoods. Much of the recognisable Parisian cityscape dates from this transformation.

Modern Paris

Paris continued changing through the Belle Époque, world exhibitions, the growth of the Métro, the two world wars and the cultural developments of the 20th and 21st centuries. Historic districts now stand alongside modern museums, offices and cultural venues.

The official Paris Tourist Office describes Paris's history from the Parisii and Roman period through the Middle Ages, Renaissance, Revolution, Haussmann's transformation and the modern city.

3. Best Places to Visit in Paris

Eiffel Tower

Paris's most recognisable landmark can be admired from Trocadéro and Champ de Mars or visited from inside. Ticket types and opening arrangements can change, so check the official Eiffel Tower website before booking.

Louvre Museum

The Louvre contains an enormous collection. Choose a few galleries or themes rather than trying to see everything in one visit.

Notre-Dame and Île de la Cité

Île de la Cité is one of historic Paris's central areas. Notre-Dame remains one of the city's defining landmarks, surrounded by streets and riverside walks.

Arc de Triomphe and Champs-Élysées

The Arc de Triomphe stands at the western end of the Champs-Élysées and makes a natural starting point for exploring this part of central Paris.

Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur

Montmartre is known for its artistic history, steep streets and village-like atmosphere. Sacré-Cœur sits at the top of the hill with broad city views.

Musée d'Orsay

Housed in a former railway station, the Musée d'Orsay is especially important for visitors interested in Impressionist and Post-Impressionist art.

Luxembourg Gardens

The Jardin du Luxembourg is a good place to slow down between museums and historic sights.

Seine River

A walk beside the Seine can connect several landmarks for free. A river cruise provides another perspective on Notre-Dame, the Louvre and the Eiffel Tower.

4. Complete Paris Itinerary

Day 1 — Eiffel Tower and Seine

  1. Start at Trocadéro.
  2. Visit the Eiffel Tower if you have a suitable ticket.
  3. Walk around Champ de Mars.
  4. Have lunch nearby.
  5. Walk beside the Seine.
  6. Take an evening cruise or enjoy a riverside walk.

Day 2 — Louvre and central Paris

  1. Begin at the Louvre.
  2. Choose selected galleries.
  3. Walk through the Tuileries Garden.
  4. Continue toward Place de la Concorde.
  5. Explore nearby streets and cafés.

Day 3 — Notre-Dame, Latin Quarter and Luxembourg Gardens

  1. Explore Île de la Cité.
  2. See Notre-Dame and follow current visitor rules.
  3. Walk through the Latin Quarter.
  4. Have lunch locally.
  5. Continue to Luxembourg Gardens.
  6. Spend the evening around Saint-Germain-des-Prés.

Day 4 — Montmartre and Arc de Triomphe

  1. Start early in Montmartre.
  2. Visit Sacré-Cœur.
  3. Explore the surrounding streets.
  4. Have lunch in the area.
  5. Travel to the Arc de Triomphe.
  6. Walk part of the Champs-Élysées.

Day 5 — Flexible culture or day trip

Use the fifth day for a museum you missed, shopping, a slower neighbourhood day or a day trip such as Versailles.

6. Where to Stay in Paris

1st–4th Arrondissements

Very convenient for major sights and historic Paris, although accommodation can be expensive.

5th Arrondissement

The Latin Quarter offers historic streets, restaurants, cafés and central access.

6th–7th Arrondissements

Saint-Germain-des-Prés and areas around the Eiffel Tower suit visitors seeking a classic Paris setting.

8th Arrondissement

Convenient for the Arc de Triomphe, Champs-Élysées and shopping.

18th Arrondissement

Montmartre offers artistic history and a different atmosphere. Check the exact hotel location because neighbourhood character varies by street.

Hotel tip: Compare location, guest reviews, cancellation terms, breakfast and transport access rather than choosing only by headline price.

7. Food & Local Experience

Food can be part of the sightseeing experience. You do not need an expensive restaurant every day.

  • Try a croissant or pain au chocolat from a bakery.
  • Have a traditional French meal at a local restaurant.
  • Visit a food market for cheese, bread, fruit and prepared foods.
  • Try crêpes at a suitable casual restaurant.
  • Leave time for a café stop.
  • Walk through a neighbourhood without making a famous landmark the destination.

8. Transportation in Paris

Metro

The Métro is one of the most practical ways to move around central Paris and is often faster than travelling by car.

RER

RER trains are useful for destinations outside central Paris, including regional attractions and some airport journeys.

Bus

Buses can be slower but allow you to see more of the city while travelling.

Walking

Walking is one of the best ways to understand Paris. Group nearby attractions together to reduce unnecessary transport journeys.

Airport transfers

Paris is served by Charles de Gaulle and Orly airports. Public transport, taxis and private transfers are available; check current official routes and fares before travelling.

9. Best Time to Visit Paris

Spring

Good for gardens, walking and outdoor sightseeing. Pack layers because weather can change.

Summer

Long days and lively streets can be appealing, while visitor demand and accommodation prices may be higher.

Autumn

A good season for museums, cafés and walks. September and October can be attractive, although conditions vary by year.

Winter

Useful for museum-focused trips and travellers who prefer a different atmosphere. Outdoor sightseeing days are shorter.

Best strategy: choose dates according to your priorities. There is no single perfect month for every traveller.

10. Practical Travel Tips

  • Reserve major attractions in advance when timed entry is available.
  • Keep your phone charged for maps and digital tickets.
  • Wear comfortable walking shoes.
  • Keep valuables secure in crowded tourist areas and public transport.
  • Check museum and monument opening days before planning.
  • Keep some flexibility for weather and closures.
  • Learn basic phrases such as “Bonjour” and “Merci.”
  • Check current transport fares before buying passes.
  • Check passport, visa and entry requirements well before departure.
Entry requirements: Visa and Schengen rules can change. Verify current requirements with official French, EU or government sources before departure.

11. Nearby / Day Trips from Paris

Versailles

One of the most popular day trips, especially for travellers interested in French royal history, architecture and gardens. Allow most of a day.

Giverny

Associated with Claude Monet and his gardens. Check seasonal opening information before planning.

Fontainebleau

An alternative historic palace experience for travellers wanting something beyond central Paris.

Disneyland Paris

Families and theme-park visitors may prefer to dedicate a full day to the resort.

12. Paris Travel FAQs

How many days do I need in Paris?

Four to five days is a practical first-trip target. Two or three days can work with careful prioritisation.

Is Paris expensive?

It can be, especially for accommodation and popular attractions, but travellers can control costs through neighbourhood choice, public transport, casual food and selective ticket purchases.

Should I stay in central Paris?

Central accommodation reduces sightseeing travel time. A hotel farther out but close to a reliable Metro or RER connection can provide another balance of price and convenience.

Can I explore Paris without a car?

Yes. Paris has extensive public transport and many central sights are comfortable to connect on foot.

Do I need to book the Eiffel Tower?

Advance planning is recommended for popular dates and summit access. Check the official Eiffel Tower website for current availability and rules.

What should I pack?

Comfortable shoes, seasonal layers, travel documents and weather-appropriate protection such as a compact rain jacket are useful.

Final Thoughts

Paris is easier to enjoy when it is not treated as a checklist. Choose a few major sights, group them by area, leave time for walking and meals, and keep part of the itinerary flexible.

The city's history is as important as its landmarks. A walk around Île de la Cité, the Latin Quarter, the Seine or Montmartre can add context to the famous sights.

Before booking, recheck attraction schedules, transport fares and entry requirements because these details can change.
