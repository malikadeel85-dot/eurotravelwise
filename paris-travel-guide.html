Paris Through Time

Complete History of Paris

Paris has a history spanning more than two millennia, with the Seine playing a central role in the city's development.

1. Before Roman Paris

The Paris area was associated with the Parisii, a Celtic people whose settlement developed around the Seine. The river provided an important route for movement, trade and communication. The historic relationship between the city and the Seine remains one of the defining features of Paris today.

2. Roman Lutetia

Under Roman rule, the settlement became known as Lutetia. Development grew around the Seine and the Left Bank, while Île de la Cité became increasingly important. Archaeological remains in the city, including the ancient baths and the Arènes de Lutèce area, preserve evidence of Roman-era Paris.

By the fourth century, the fortified Île de la Cité had become a strategic centre as the Roman Empire faced increasing military pressure.

3. The Medieval City

During the Middle Ages, Paris developed into a major political, religious and intellectual centre. The Île de la Cité became an important heart of the city, while areas on both banks of the Seine expanded.

Construction of Notre-Dame began in 1163, marking one of the most important chapters in the medieval architectural history of Paris. The surrounding city continued to grow through churches, markets, bridges, schools and commercial districts.

4. Paris Becomes a Major European Capital

Over the following centuries, Paris became increasingly important to the French monarchy and central government. Royal buildings, churches, squares and institutions shaped the city and strengthened its role as the political and cultural centre of France.

5. The French Revolution

Paris was at the centre of the French Revolution beginning in 1789. Political demonstrations, popular movements and major events transformed France and affected the entire city.

Places such as the Bastille area, Place de la Concorde and other historic sites can still be connected to this period when exploring Paris.

6. Napoleon and Nineteenth-Century Paris

The nineteenth century brought enormous changes. Paris expanded and modernised, while new monuments, public buildings, railway connections and boulevards changed how people moved through the city.

7. Haussmann's Transformation

Under Napoleon III, Baron Georges-Eugène Haussmann directed a major programme of urban restructuring. Many narrow medieval streets were replaced or connected by broad boulevards, squares and new urban infrastructure.

The Haussmannian buildings and wide avenues that visitors associate with Paris today are a major legacy of this transformation. UNESCO notes that Haussmann's urban planning influenced city planning beyond France.

8. The Eiffel Tower and the Modern Image of Paris

The Eiffel Tower was built for the 1889 Exposition Universelle. It became one of the most recognisable symbols of Paris and remains a major part of the city's visual identity.

9. Paris in the Twentieth Century

The twentieth century brought world wars, artistic movements, political change and rapid modernisation. Paris remained a major centre for art, literature, fashion, architecture and international culture.

10. Paris Today

Modern Paris combines historic districts with contemporary transport, business, cultural institutions and tourism. The Seine remains a central part of the city's geography and heritage.

The Banks of the Seine in central Paris were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1991. The protected area brings together monuments and urban landscapes ranging from medieval architecture to nineteenth- and twentieth-century landmarks.

History tip: One of the best ways to understand Paris is to walk along the Seine between Île de la Cité, the Louvre and the Eiffel Tower. The route connects different periods of the city's architectural and urban history.

Planning Your Days

Complete Paris Itinerary

This itinerary is designed for a first-time visitor. You can shorten or expand it depending on your interests and museum bookings.

Day 1 — Historic Paris

Île de la Cité, Notre-Dame, Latin Quarter & Seine

Start around Île de la Cité. Visit Notre-Dame and explore the surrounding historic streets. Continue towards the Latin Quarter and walk beside the Seine in the afternoon.

  • Île de la Cité
  • Notre-Dame
  • Sainte-Chapelle area
  • Latin Quarter
  • Seine river walk
Day 2 — Louvre & Central Paris

Louvre, Tuileries & Champs-Élysées

Reserve enough time for the Louvre rather than trying to see everything. After the museum, walk through the Tuileries Garden and continue towards Place de la Concorde and the Champs-Élysées.

  • Louvre Museum
  • Tuileries Garden
  • Place de la Concorde
  • Champs-Élysées
  • Arc de Triomphe area
Day 3 — Eiffel Tower & Left Bank

Eiffel Tower, Invalides & Saint-Germain

Spend the morning around the Eiffel Tower. Continue towards Les Invalides and then explore the Left Bank. Finish the day with dinner in the Saint-Germain-des-Prés area.

  • Eiffel Tower
  • Champ de Mars
  • Les Invalides
  • Saint-Germain-des-Prés
  • Evening Seine views
Day 4 — Montmartre & Artistic Paris

Sacré-Cœur, Montmartre & Evening Paris

Explore Montmartre in the morning, including Sacré-Cœur and the surrounding streets. Take your time with cafés, small shops and neighbourhood views.

  • Sacré-Cœur
  • Montmartre streets
  • Place du Tertre
  • Local cafés
  • Evening neighbourhood walk
Day 5 — Flexible Paris

Museums, Shopping, Versailles or a Relaxed Day

Use a fifth day according to your interests. You can visit another museum, explore Le Marais, take a day trip to Versailles, shop, enjoy a Seine cruise or simply slow down and experience local neighbourhoods.

Budget Planning

Paris Travel Cost Guide

Paris can be visited on different budgets. Your biggest variable costs will usually be accommodation, dining, attractions and transport.

Travel Style Typical Approach Planning Idea
Budget Simple accommodation, bakeries, supermarkets, public transport and selected paid attractions. Focus on free walks, parks and neighbourhoods.
Mid-range Comfortable hotel, restaurants, public transport and several major attractions. Reserve important attractions in advance.
Premium Central hotel, taxis/private transfers, premium dining and more guided experiences. Allow flexibility for reservations and experiences.
Transport example for 2026: Île-de-France Mobilités lists the full-price Metro-Train-RER single ticket at €2.55 and the Bus-Tram single ticket at €2.05. Airport travel tickets and tourist passes have separate prices. Transport prices can change, so check the official fare page before travelling.

Attractions, restaurants and accommodation prices vary by date, season, availability and booking conditions. Use current provider prices when building your final trip budget.

Getting Around

Transportation in Paris

Paris has a dense public transport network covering the city and the wider Île-de-France region.

🚇 Metro

The Metro is one of the most practical ways to move around central Paris. The official Paris tourism office describes a network of 16 Metro lines and more than 300 stations.

🚆 RER

RER trains are particularly useful for destinations outside central Paris, including major regional attractions.

🚌 Bus & Tram

Buses and trams provide additional routes and can be useful when you want to see the city rather than travel underground.

🚲 Cycling

Cycling can be an enjoyable option for travellers who are comfortable with city traffic and dedicated cycling infrastructure.

🚕 Taxi & Ride Services

Useful when carrying luggage, travelling late or moving between places where public transport is less convenient.

🚶 Walking

Walking is one of the best ways to understand Paris because many historic areas are close enough to connect on foot.

2026 transport change: Paris and Île-de-France are moving away from traditional paper tickets. Digital ticketing and Navigo solutions are increasingly important. Check the current official fare and ticket information before your trip.
Timing Matters

Best Time to Visit Paris

There is no single perfect month for every traveller. Your choice depends on weather preferences, crowds, events and budget.

🌸 Spring

A popular period for parks, walking and sightseeing. Plan ahead for popular attractions and accommodation.

☀️ Summer

Longer daylight hours can be useful for sightseeing, although popular periods can bring greater demand and higher accommodation prices.

🍂 Autumn

A good season for museums, neighbourhood walks and cultural activities, with changing weather requiring flexible planning.

❄️ Winter

Shorter days can work well for museum-focused trips and city breaks. Pack for cool weather and check attraction schedules.

Practical Advice

Practical Paris Travel Tips

Before You Go

  • Check passport and visa requirements for your nationality.
  • Confirm hotel cancellation conditions.
  • Reserve high-demand attractions when necessary.
  • Save important addresses offline.
  • Check current public transport fares.
  • Consider travel insurance.

While You Are There

  • Keep valuables secure in crowded tourist areas.
  • Use official ticketing channels whenever possible.
  • Allow extra time between attractions.
  • Wear comfortable shoes.
  • Respect local rules at museums and monuments.
  • Keep emergency and accommodation information accessible.
Important: Visa, border and entry rules depend on your nationality and can change. Check the official French government sources before travelling rather than relying only on a travel blog.

Questions

Paris Travel FAQs

How many days do you need in Paris?

Four to five days is a useful starting point for a first visit because it gives you time for major landmarks, museums, neighbourhoods and a more relaxed day.

Is Paris expensive?

Paris can be expensive, but travellers can control costs through accommodation choices, public transport, casual meals, free attractions and careful booking.

Is public transport useful in Paris?

Yes. Metro, RER, buses and trams form a dense network. Walking can also be combined with public transport for many sightseeing days.

Should I book attractions in advance?

For major attractions and popular dates, advance booking can help avoid unnecessary waiting and sold-out time slots.

What is the best area to stay in Paris?

It depends on your priorities. Le Marais, the Latin Quarter, Saint-Germain, Montmartre and areas around the Eiffel Tower can all suit different travel styles.

Can Paris be explored without a car?

Yes. For most sightseeing trips, public transport and walking provide practical ways to explore the city without renting a car.

Is Paris good for a first European trip?

Paris offers a combination of famous landmarks, museums, history, architecture, food and transport connections, making it a practical destination for many types of city trips.

How can I save money in Paris?

Compare accommodation areas, use public transport, mix paid attractions with free walks and public spaces, and plan major bookings before arrival.
