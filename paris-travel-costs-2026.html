Paris Travel Costs 2026: Daily Budget, Prices & Money-Saving Tips

How much does a trip to Paris cost in 2026? This practical guide explains transport prices, attraction costs, food and accommodation planning ranges, sample daily budgets and simple ways to control your spending.

Paris can fit different travel budgets. Your total cost depends mainly on where you stay, how often you use paid attractions, how you eat and whether you travel during a busy period. The best approach is to build your budget by category rather than relying on one fixed “Paris price.”

2026 price note: Transport figures in this guide use official 2026 Île-de-France Mobilités fares. Attraction prices can change, so check the official ticket page before booking.

2. Paris Transport Costs in 2026

Public transport is one of the easiest ways to control a Paris budget. Île-de-France Mobilités lists the 2026 full-price Metro-Train-RER ticket at €2.55. The full-price Bus-Tram ticket is €2.05. A Paris Region–airports ticket is €14 at full price.

3. Attraction Costs to Include in Your Budget

Paid attractions can become one of the biggest parts of a Paris trip budget. As examples, the official Paris Tourist Office ticketing service currently lists Musée d'Orsay tickets from €16, Arc de Triomphe from €16 and Seine cruises from around €17. Prices and availability can change.

The Louvre's official information states that, from 14 January 2026, the general admission price is €22 for eligible EEA visitors and €32 for visitors who are not residents/citizens of the EEA. Time-slot booking is recommended.

4. Food Budget in Paris

Food spending is highly flexible. A traveller trying to keep costs down can mix bakeries, supermarkets, casual takeaway meals and inexpensive cafés. A mid-range traveller can budget more for sit-down lunches and dinners, while fine dining can increase the daily total considerably.

Simple ways to control food costs

  • Use a bakery or casual café for breakfast instead of a hotel breakfast every day.
  • Buy water and snacks from supermarkets when practical.
  • Have your main restaurant meal at lunch when menus are more suitable for your budget.
  • Mix restaurant meals with simple meals on busy sightseeing days.
  • Check the menu and service charges before ordering.

5. Accommodation: Where Your Budget Changes Most

Hotel prices vary heavily by neighbourhood, dates, room type and demand. Instead of assuming one fixed nightly rate, compare the total price for your exact dates and include taxes or mandatory charges shown during booking.

For a lower-cost trip, consider staying slightly outside the most expensive central areas while remaining close to a convenient Metro or RER station. For a first-time visit, saving a little on location is only useful if the accommodation still has practical transport connections.

7. How to Save Money in Paris

  • Walk whenever practical: Many central sights are close enough to combine into one walking day.
  • Use public transport: Compare individual fares with day or tourist passes based on your actual itinerary.
  • Book major attractions ahead: This can help with availability, especially for popular museums and timed-entry attractions.
  • Mix free and paid sights: Parks, neighbourhood walks, viewpoints and exterior visits can add variety without another admission fee.
  • Stay near transport: A slightly less central hotel can be worthwhile if it has convenient Metro/RER connections.
  • Set a daily spending limit: Separate accommodation from your daily sightseeing and food allowance.

8. Is Paris Expensive?

Paris can be expensive, but the cost is not fixed. Accommodation and paid experiences can raise the total quickly, while walking, public transport, simple meals and a selective attraction list can make a trip much more manageable.

Best budgeting method: decide your accommodation first, estimate your daily food budget, then add transport and only the attractions you genuinely want to visit. This gives you a much more useful Paris budget than a single city-wide average.
