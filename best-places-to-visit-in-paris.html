Best Places to Visit in Paris: Complete Travel Guide

Discover Paris beyond the famous landmarks. This complete guide explores the city's history, iconic attractions, neighborhoods, food, transportation, travel costs, itinerary ideas, day trips and practical tips for planning your trip.

1. Introduction to Paris

Paris is one of Europe's most famous cultural and historic cities. Located along the Seine River, the French capital combines centuries of history with museums, architecture, food, fashion, parks and lively neighborhoods.

For a first visit, Paris can feel overwhelming because there is far more to see than can fit into a single weekend. The best approach is to group attractions by neighborhood and plan enough time for both famous landmarks and quieter local experiences.

This guide is designed for first-time visitors as well as travelers returning to Paris who want to explore the city in greater depth.

Quick answer: If you are visiting Paris for the first time, consider allowing at least 4 to 5 days. This gives you enough time to experience major landmarks while also leaving room for neighborhoods, food and a possible day trip.

2. Complete History of Paris

Paris has a history stretching back more than two thousand years. Its story is closely connected with the Seine River and the settlement associated with the Parisii, a Gallic people whose name became connected with the city.

Ancient Paris

The earliest development of Paris was closely connected with the Seine and the Île de la Cité. Roman influence later shaped the settlement, leaving archaeological and architectural traces that can still be explored in the city.

Medieval Paris

During the Middle Ages, Paris grew into an important political, religious and intellectual center. Notre-Dame, Sainte-Chapelle and other historic structures reflect this period.

The medieval city developed around the Seine and gradually expanded on both banks of the river.

The Renaissance and Royal Paris

During the Renaissance and later royal periods, Paris experienced major architectural and cultural changes. The Louvre evolved from a medieval fortress into a royal palace and eventually became one of the world's best-known museums.

The French Revolution

The French Revolution beginning in 1789 transformed France politically and socially. Paris became one of the central locations of revolutionary events.

19th-Century Paris

The 19th century dramatically changed the appearance of Paris. Baron Georges-Eugène Haussmann oversaw major urban changes that created broad boulevards, redesigned public spaces and influenced the distinctive appearance of central Paris today.

The Eiffel Tower and the Modern City

The Eiffel Tower was constructed for the 1889 World's Fair. Its structure was completed in March 1889 and it opened to visitors during the World's Fair later that year.

Today the tower is one of the most recognizable symbols of Paris, while the city continues to combine historic districts with modern architecture, museums, cultural institutions and contemporary neighborhoods.

3. Best Places to Visit in Paris

1. Eiffel Tower

The Eiffel Tower is the landmark most closely associated with Paris. Visitors can admire it from surrounding streets and gardens or purchase tickets to access its viewing levels.

For photographs, the area around Trocadéro provides some of the city's classic views of the tower.

2. Louvre Museum

The Louvre is one of the world's most famous museums and occupies the historic Louvre palace complex.

A first visit should be planned carefully because the collection is enormous. Rather than trying to see everything, choose a few departments and major works that interest you.

3. Notre-Dame Cathedral

Notre-Dame stands on the Île de la Cité and is one of the most important landmarks in Paris. Its Gothic architecture and location beside the Seine make the surrounding area an essential stop for visitors interested in the history of Paris.

4. Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur

Montmartre is known for its artistic history, winding streets and elevated views over Paris. The Basilica of the Sacré-Cœur sits at the top of the hill.

Allow several hours to walk through the neighborhood rather than visiting only the basilica.

5. Arc de Triomphe

The Arc de Triomphe stands at the western end of the Champs-Élysées and provides an excellent viewpoint over several major Parisian avenues.

6. Champs-Élysées

The Champs-Élysées is one of Paris's best-known avenues. It connects the area around the Arc de Triomphe with Place de la Concorde.

7. Musée d'Orsay

Musée d'Orsay is particularly important for visitors interested in Impressionist and Post-Impressionist art. The museum occupies a former railway station beside the Seine.

8. Sainte-Chapelle

Sainte-Chapelle is famous for its spectacular stained-glass windows and Gothic architecture. It is located on the Île de la Cité, making it easy to combine with Notre-Dame and other historic sites.

9. Seine River

Walking along the Seine is one of the simplest ways to experience Paris. A river cruise can provide another perspective of landmarks including Notre-Dame, the Louvre and the Eiffel Tower.

10. Luxembourg Gardens

The Luxembourg Gardens provide a peaceful break from busy sightseeing. The gardens are particularly suitable for travelers who want a slower afternoon in Paris.

4. Complete Paris Itinerary

Day 1 — Historic Paris

  1. Start around Île de la Cité.
  2. Visit Notre-Dame and explore the surrounding streets.
  3. Visit Sainte-Chapelle if time allows.
  4. Walk toward the Louvre area.
  5. Finish the evening with a walk beside the Seine.

Day 2 — Eiffel Tower and Central Paris

  1. Begin near Trocadéro.
  2. Walk toward the Eiffel Tower.
  3. Explore Champ de Mars.
  4. Continue toward the Seine.
  5. Visit Musée d'Orsay or another museum.
  6. Enjoy dinner in a neighborhood restaurant.

Day 3 — Louvre and Central Paris

  1. Spend the morning at the Louvre.
  2. Walk through the Tuileries Garden.
  3. Continue toward Place de la Concorde.
  4. Explore the Champs-Élysées.
  5. Finish at the Arc de Triomphe.

Day 4 — Montmartre

  1. Start in Montmartre.
  2. Visit Sacré-Cœur.
  3. Explore the neighborhood on foot.
  4. Have lunch at a local café.
  5. Explore nearby streets and viewpoints.
  6. Enjoy a relaxed evening.

Day 5 — Flexible Paris Day

Use the fifth day for anything missed during the first four days. Good options include another museum, shopping, a Seine cruise, Luxembourg Gardens, Le Marais or a day trip outside Paris.

5. Paris Travel Cost

The cost of visiting Paris depends heavily on accommodation, travel season, restaurant choices and the number of paid attractions you visit.

Expense Budget Approach Comfortable Approach
Accommodation Hostel / budget room Mid-range hotel
Food Supermarkets, bakeries and casual meals Cafés and restaurants
Transport Metro, walking and buses Metro plus occasional taxi
Attractions Mix of free and paid attractions Several museums and monuments

As a planning rule, travelers should create separate budgets for accommodation, food, local transportation and attractions rather than treating Paris as one fixed daily cost.

Transportation note: In 2026, the official Île-de-France Mobilités Metro-Train-RER ticket is €2.55 at full price. Airport journeys use different airport fares, so check the current official fare before traveling.

6. Where to Stay in Paris

Choosing the right neighborhood can make a major difference to your trip. Paris is divided into arrondissements, and each area has its own character.

Le Marais

A popular area for travelers who want historic streets, cafés, restaurants and a lively atmosphere.

Latin Quarter

A convenient choice for visitors interested in historic Paris, the Seine and central attractions.

Saint-Germain-des-Prés

Known for cafés, boutiques, galleries and a classic Left Bank atmosphere.

Montmartre

Suitable for travelers who prefer a more neighborhood-oriented atmosphere and want to be close to Sacré-Cœur.

Near the Eiffel Tower

Useful for travelers who specifically want easy access to the Eiffel Tower and surrounding attractions, although prices can vary significantly.

7. Food & Local Experience

Paris is as much about food culture as sightseeing. Visitors should leave some time in their itinerary for bakeries, cafés, markets and neighborhood restaurants.

Experience Paris Like a Local

Instead of spending every hour moving between famous attractions, choose one neighborhood and explore it slowly. Stop at a café, visit a local market and walk through side streets.

Local experience tip: A relaxed walk along the Seine around sunset can be one of the most memorable free experiences in Paris.

8. Transportation in Paris

Paris has an extensive public transport network including the Metro, RER trains, buses and trams. Walking is also extremely useful in central areas.

Metro

The Metro is one of the easiest ways to move around the city. It is especially useful when attractions are located in different neighborhoods.

RER

RER trains are useful for destinations outside central Paris, including some major day-trip locations.

Bus

Buses can be useful when you want to see the city while traveling rather than spending the entire journey underground.

Walking

Many central neighborhoods are best explored on foot. Walking also allows you to discover streets and small areas that are easy to miss when using public transportation.

Airport Transportation

Paris has several airport transportation options. Airport fares are different from standard central Paris tickets, so check the official Île-de-France Mobilités information before your journey.

9. Best Time to Visit Paris

Spring

Spring can be a pleasant period for walking, gardens and outdoor sightseeing. Weather can change quickly, so a light waterproof layer is useful.

Summer

Summer brings long days and strong tourist demand. Popular attractions can be busy, so booking ahead is often sensible.

Autumn

Autumn can offer a comfortable combination of sightseeing weather and seasonal atmosphere. September and October can be particularly attractive for travelers who prefer cooler temperatures.

Winter

Winter can be quieter than the busiest tourist periods, although daylight hours are shorter. Museums and indoor attractions become particularly useful.

Best overall strategy: Rather than choosing a single "perfect" month, consider your priorities. Choose warmer months for long outdoor days and gardens, and cooler periods if museums and indoor attractions are more important.

10. Practical Travel Tips

  • Book popular attractions ahead when timed entry is available.
  • Wear comfortable shoes because Paris is best experienced partly on foot.
  • Keep your belongings secure in crowded tourist areas and on public transportation.
  • Check official attraction websites for opening hours before visiting.
  • Do not try to visit every major attraction in one day.
  • Keep some flexibility in your itinerary for weather and unexpected discoveries.
  • Learn a few simple French phrases such as "Bonjour", "Merci" and "S'il vous plaît".
  • Keep digital and physical copies of important travel documents.
  • Use official transport information when checking current fares.
  • Check museum and monument rules before arrival, especially regarding bags and security.

11. Nearby Places and Day Trips from Paris

Palace of Versailles

Versailles is one of the most popular excursions from Paris. The palace and gardens provide an opportunity to explore France's royal history outside the capital.

Giverny

Giverny is associated with Claude Monet and is particularly interesting for travelers interested in Impressionist art and gardens.

Disneyland Paris

Disneyland Paris is an option for families and theme-park enthusiasts. Allow a full day if you want to experience the major attractions without rushing.

Fontainebleau

Fontainebleau offers another historical excursion from Paris, combining a famous château with a large surrounding forest.

Reims

Reims can be considered for travelers interested in French history, architecture and the Champagne region.

12. Frequently Asked Questions About Paris

How many days do I need in Paris?

Four to five days is a useful starting point for first-time visitors. A shorter trip is possible, but you will need to prioritize attractions.

What is the best place to visit in Paris for first-time visitors?

The Eiffel Tower, Louvre, Notre-Dame area, Montmartre, Arc de Triomphe and Seine are among the major places many first-time visitors include.

Is Paris expensive?

Paris can be expensive, particularly for accommodation and popular attractions. Travelers can reduce costs by using public transportation, walking, choosing casual food options and mixing paid attractions with free experiences.

Is public transportation easy in Paris?

Yes. The Metro, RER, buses and other transport services connect many parts of the city and surrounding region. Check current fares and routes before traveling.

Can I visit Paris without speaking French?

Yes. Many visitors travel successfully without speaking French. However, learning a few basic French greetings is useful and shows courtesy.

What should I book in advance in Paris?

Consider booking popular attractions, timed museum entries, special tours and accommodation in advance, especially during busy travel periods.

Is Paris suitable for a family trip?

Yes. Families can choose museums, parks, boat trips, landmarks and attractions suited to children. The best itinerary depends on the children's ages and interests.

Final Thoughts

Paris is not a city that needs to be rushed. Its most famous landmarks deserve a place in your itinerary, but some of the best memories can come from walking through historic neighborhoods, sitting at a café, exploring a market or watching the Seine at sunset.

For a first trip, focus on a manageable group of attractions and organize them by area. Leave enough time to experience the city rather than simply checking places off a list.

Whether you are planning a weekend, a five-day holiday or a longer European journey, Paris can be a strong starting point for exploring France and the wider Europe region.
