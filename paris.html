Paris Travel Guide

Best Places to Visit in Paris

Paris is one of Europe's great historic cities, combining world-famous monuments, museums, neighbourhoods, food, architecture and the River Seine. This complete EuroTravelWise guide covers the history of Paris, the best attractions, a practical 3-day itinerary, travel costs, where to stay, food, transportation, day trips and useful planning advice.

1. Introduction

Paris is the capital of France and one of Europe's most important cultural, historical and artistic destinations. The city is especially famous for the Eiffel Tower, Louvre Museum, Notre-Dame Cathedral, historic boulevards, cafés, museums, gardens and the River Seine.

A first trip to Paris can feel overwhelming because there is much more to see than can realistically fit into one day. The best approach is to group attractions by area instead of repeatedly crossing the city.

For a first visit, three days provides enough time to experience several major landmarks while still leaving room for neighbourhood walks, cafés and relaxed exploration. Four or five days allows additional museums, Versailles or other day trips.

2. Complete History of Paris

Paris has more than two thousand years of history. The development of the city is closely connected with the Seine, which provided transportation, trade, defence and a natural geographical centre for the growing settlement.

Ancient Paris and the Parisii

Before Roman rule, the area was associated with the Parisii, a Gaulish people whose settlement developed around the Seine. Roman control later transformed the area, and the settlement known as Lutetia developed on and around the Left Bank and Île de la Cité.

Medieval Paris

During the Middle Ages, Paris became an important political, religious and intellectual centre. The city expanded around the Île de la Cité and the Left Bank, while churches, markets, schools and defensive structures shaped its urban development.

The construction of Notre-Dame began in the 12th century. The Louvre also began its life as a defensive fortress before later becoming a royal residence and, eventually, a museum.

The Renaissance and Royal Paris

During the Renaissance and following centuries, Paris was increasingly shaped by royal building projects, new streets, squares, palaces and cultural institutions. The Louvre developed from a fortress into a major royal complex.

The French Revolution

The French Revolution beginning in 1789 profoundly changed Paris and France. The city became a major centre of political activity and revolutionary events. Many places that visitors see today are connected to this period of French history.

Napoleon and the 19th Century

Paris expanded significantly during the 19th century. Industrialisation, population growth, new transport systems and major public works changed the city's appearance.

Haussmann's Transformation

One of the most important changes came during the 19th century under Baron Georges-Eugène Haussmann. Wide avenues, parks, public buildings and improved infrastructure transformed large parts of Paris.

Many of the broad avenues and characteristic Parisian streetscapes visitors recognise today are connected to this period of urban transformation.

The Eiffel Tower and World Exhibitions

The Eiffel Tower was constructed for the 1889 Exposition Universelle, held during the centenary period of the French Revolution. Construction began in January 1887 and the tower was completed on 31 March 1889.

Paris in the 20th and 21st Centuries

Paris continued to develop through the 20th century, becoming an international centre for art, fashion, literature, food, diplomacy and tourism.

Today, the historic relationship between the city and the Seine remains especially important. The Banks of the Seine area is recognised by UNESCO as a World Heritage site and includes major landmarks ranging from the Louvre and Notre-Dame to the Eiffel Tower.

3. Best Places to Visit in Paris

Iconic Landmark

Eiffel Tower

The Eiffel Tower is the landmark most strongly associated with Paris. It was completed in 1889 for the Exposition Universelle and remains one of the city's most recognisable attractions.

Visitors can experience the tower itself or enjoy views of it from places such as Trocadéro and the Champ de Mars. Sunset and evening are popular times for views and photography.

Museum & History

Louvre Museum

The Louvre has a remarkable history. It began as a fortress in the medieval period, later became a royal palace and was opened as a public museum in 1793.

The museum's collections cover thousands of years of art and include famous works such as the Mona Lisa and the Venus de Milo.

Because the Louvre is enormous, visitors should choose a small number of galleries and masterpieces rather than trying to see everything in one visit.

Historic Cathedral

Notre-Dame de Paris

Notre-Dame stands on the Île de la Cité and is one of the best-known Gothic cathedrals in the world. Construction began in 1163 and continued over many decades.

The cathedral has played an important role in the religious and cultural history of Paris and France. Its restoration after the 2019 fire became one of the major heritage projects of modern Paris.

Historic Centre

Île de la Cité

The Île de la Cité is one of the historic hearts of Paris. Its location in the Seine makes it an excellent starting point for exploring Notre-Dame, Sainte- Chapelle and the surrounding historic centre.

Historic Architecture

Sainte-Chapelle

Sainte-Chapelle is famous for its Gothic architecture and extensive stained-glass windows. It is particularly valuable for travellers interested in medieval Paris, religious history and architecture.

Art & Culture

Musée d'Orsay

The Musée d'Orsay is located in a former railway station and is especially known for its collection of 19th- and early 20th-century art, including major Impressionist and Post-Impressionist works.

Neighbourhood

Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur

Montmartre is known for its hilltop setting, artistic history, narrow streets and views across Paris. The Basilica of Sacré-Cœur sits at the top of the hill.

Allow several hours for the neighbourhood rather than visiting only the basilica. Walking through the streets, small squares and viewpoints is part of the experience.

Historic Avenue

Champs-Élysées and Arc de Triomphe

The Champs-Élysées is one of Paris's most famous avenues. At its western end is the Arc de Triomphe, a major monument connected with French military history.

Parisian Experience

Seine River

The Seine is more than a river running through Paris. It is part of the city's historical identity and connects many major landmarks.

A walk beside the river or a Seine cruise can provide a different perspective of the Louvre, Notre-Dame, historic bridges and Eiffel Tower.

Garden

Luxembourg Gardens

The Luxembourg Gardens are a pleasant place to slow down after visiting museums and monuments. The gardens are especially suitable for travellers who want a break from the busy central streets.

4. Complete 3-Day Paris Itinerary

This itinerary is designed for a first-time visitor. It can be adjusted according to museum opening days, ticket availability, weather and personal interests.

Day 1 — Eiffel Tower, Seine and Historic Paris

  1. Start around Trocadéro for Eiffel Tower views.
  2. Visit the Eiffel Tower if you have a reserved time.
  3. Walk through the Champ de Mars.
  4. Continue toward the Seine.
  5. Visit Île de la Cité.
  6. See Notre-Dame.
  7. Visit Sainte-Chapelle if time allows.
  8. Walk through the historic centre.
  9. Finish the evening with a Seine-side walk or cruise.

Day 2 — Louvre, Tuileries and Champs-Élysées

  1. Start early at the Louvre.
  2. Focus on selected galleries rather than trying to see everything.
  3. Walk through the Tuileries Garden.
  4. Continue toward Place de la Concorde.
  5. Walk along the Champs-Élysées.
  6. Visit the Arc de Triomphe.
  7. Enjoy the evening in central Paris.

Day 3 — Montmartre, Museums and Local Paris

  1. Start in Montmartre.
  2. Visit Sacré-Cœur.
  3. Explore the surrounding streets and viewpoints.
  4. Have lunch in the neighbourhood.
  5. Visit Musée d'Orsay or another museum.
  6. Explore Saint-Germain-des-Prés or the Latin Quarter.
  7. Finish with a relaxed Parisian dinner.

5. Paris Travel Cost

Paris can be expensive, but the total cost depends heavily on accommodation, season, restaurant choices and how many paid attractions you visit.

Budget Traveller

Hostel or basic accommodation, inexpensive meals, walking and public transport, and a limited number of paid attractions.

Planning idea: approximately €70–€120 per person per day, excluding international flights.

Mid-Range Traveller

Comfortable hotel, restaurants, public transport, cafés and several paid attractions.

Planning idea: approximately €140–€250 per person per day, excluding international flights.

Luxury Traveller

Premium accommodation, taxis, fine dining, private tours and multiple paid experiences can increase the daily budget substantially.

Planning idea: €300+ per person per day depending on choices.

Important: These are planning ranges rather than fixed prices. Hotel rates, restaurant prices, attraction tickets and seasonal demand change. Always check the current price before booking.

6. Where to Stay in Paris

Choosing the right neighbourhood can make a major difference to a Paris trip. The best area depends on whether you prioritise sightseeing, nightlife, food, quiet streets, budget or transport connections.

1st Arrondissement — Louvre

Excellent for travellers who want to be close to major central attractions. Accommodation is generally more expensive.

4th Arrondissement — Marais and Notre-Dame

A historic and lively area with restaurants, shops, cultural attractions and excellent access to central Paris.

5th Arrondissement — Latin Quarter

A popular choice for travellers interested in historic streets, cafés and a lively atmosphere.

6th Arrondissement — Saint-Germain-des-Prés

Known for its cafés, elegant streets, galleries and central location.

7th Arrondissement — Eiffel Tower Area

Useful for travellers who specifically want to stay near the Eiffel Tower and central monuments.

18th Arrondissement — Montmartre

Good for travellers attracted to Montmartre's artistic history, hilltop views and distinctive neighbourhood atmosphere.

Hotel tip: When comparing hotels, check the distance to a metro station, recent guest reviews, cancellation conditions and the total price rather than choosing only by the hotel name.

7. Food & Local Experience

Food is an important part of the Paris experience. Travellers can experience everything from inexpensive bakeries to traditional cafés and fine dining.

Foods and experiences to try

  • Croissants and French pastries
  • Baguette sandwiches
  • Crêpes
  • French cheeses
  • Onion soup
  • Steak frites
  • French desserts
  • Local bakery breakfasts
  • Traditional cafés
  • French markets

One of the easiest ways to experience local Paris is to combine a major attraction with a neighbourhood food stop instead of spending the entire day inside museums.

8. Transportation in Paris

Metro

The Paris Metro is one of the most practical ways to move around the city. For visitors, metro stations are often more useful than judging distances only by kilometres.

RER

RER trains are useful for longer journeys within the Paris region and for several major destinations outside central Paris.

Bus

Buses can be useful when you want to see the city while travelling rather than going underground.

Walking

Many central attractions are close enough to combine on foot. Walking is also one of the best ways to discover neighbourhood architecture, cafés and smaller streets.

2026 Transport Information

From January 2026, the standard Metro-Train-RER ticket is €2.55. Île-de-France Mobilités also lists weekly, daily and other transport products. Airport travel has separate fares, so visitors should check the current official fare before travelling.

9. Best Time to Visit Paris

Spring — March to May

Spring can be a pleasant time for walking, gardens and outdoor sightseeing. Weather can still be changeable, so layers and a light rain jacket are useful.

Summer — June to August

Summer offers long daylight hours and many outdoor opportunities. It can also bring higher demand and accommodation prices.

Autumn — September to November

Autumn can be attractive for museums, cafés, architecture and city walks. September and October can be particularly useful months for combining outdoor and indoor activities.

Winter — December to February

Winter has shorter days but can work well for museum-focused trips. December also brings seasonal decorations and Christmas markets in parts of the city.

Simple rule: Choose your travel dates according to what matters most to you—weather, crowds, prices, museums, seasonal events or Christmas atmosphere.

10. Practical Travel Tips

  • Book major attractions ahead when timed entry is available.
  • Wear comfortable shoes because Paris is excellent for walking.
  • Keep valuables secure in crowded tourist areas and on public transport.
  • Check official attraction opening times before visiting.
  • Do not plan too many major museums in one day.
  • Keep some flexibility for weather changes.
  • Use public transport for longer journeys and walk between nearby attractions.
  • Check cancellation policies before booking accommodation.
  • Keep digital and paper copies of important travel documents.
  • Respect local rules at museums, churches and historic monuments.
  • When booking tours or attractions through third parties, read the cancellation and refund conditions.

11. Nearby / Day Trips from Paris

Versailles

The Palace of Versailles is one of the most popular excursions from Paris. It is especially suitable for travellers interested in French royal history, architecture and gardens.

Giverny

Giverny is associated with Claude Monet and can be an interesting day trip for travellers interested in Impressionism and gardens. Seasonal opening dates should be checked before planning.

Disneyland Paris

Disneyland Paris can be visited as a day trip from central Paris and is particularly suitable for families and theme-park fans.

Fontainebleau

Fontainebleau offers another historic royal destination and can provide an alternative to the more heavily visited Versailles experience.

12. Frequently Asked Questions

How many days do you need in Paris?

Three days is a useful starting point for a first visit. Four or five days allows more museums, neighbourhoods and a possible day trip.

What is the best place to visit in Paris for first-time visitors?

There is no single attraction that covers the entire Paris experience. A first visit usually benefits from combining major landmarks such as the Eiffel Tower, Louvre, Notre-Dame and Montmartre with neighbourhood exploration.

Is Paris expensive?

Paris can be expensive, particularly for accommodation and dining, but travellers can reduce costs through public transport, walking, bakeries, markets and careful hotel selection.

What is the best area to stay in Paris?

For a first visit, central neighbourhoods such as the 1st, 4th, 5th, 6th and 7th arrondissements can provide convenient access to major attractions. The best choice depends on budget and travel style.

Is public transport good in Paris?

Yes. The Metro, RER and bus network provide extensive coverage, while many central attractions can also be reached on foot.

Should I book Paris attractions in advance?

For popular attractions and timed-entry museums, advance booking can be useful, especially during busy periods. Always check the attraction's official booking conditions.

Can Paris be visited on a budget?

Yes. Staying outside the most expensive central areas, using public transport, walking, choosing bakeries and casual restaurants, and selecting free or lower-cost activities can reduce the total trip cost.

What should I book before travelling to Paris?

Consider booking accommodation, major timed-entry attractions, popular tours and airport transportation where necessary. Leave some free time for spontaneous exploration.

Final Thoughts

Paris is much more than a list of famous landmarks. Its history, architecture, museums, neighbourhoods, food culture and relationship with the Seine are what make the city distinctive.

For a first visit, focus on a manageable number of major attractions, group nearby places together and leave time for walking. A balanced itinerary can combine history and museums with cafés, neighbourhoods and relaxed evenings beside the Seine.

EuroTravelWise recommendation: Save this guide before your trip, check official attraction information close to your travel date, and build your daily plan around neighbourhoods rather than trying to cross Paris repeatedly.
