3 Days in Paris 2026: The Perfect First-Time Itinerary

Planning a short trip to Paris? This practical 3-day Paris itinerary covers famous landmarks, historic neighbourhoods, scenic walks, food, transport and useful tips for making the most of your first visit.

Paris is one of Europe's most popular city-break destinations, and three days can be enough to experience many of its famous landmarks while still leaving time to enjoy cafés, neighbourhoods and walks along the Seine.

This itinerary is designed for first-time visitors who want a balanced trip rather than trying to see every attraction in the city.

Day 1: Louvre, Notre-Dame and the Seine

Start your first day in central Paris with some of the city's most famous historic and cultural sights.

Morning: Louvre Museum

Begin your day at the Louvre. The museum is enormous, so avoid trying to see everything in one visit. Choose a few sections or famous works that interest you and allow enough time to explore comfortably.

Afternoon: Île de la Cité and Notre-Dame

After the Louvre, walk toward the Seine and continue to Île de la Cité. Explore the area around Notre-Dame and enjoy the historic atmosphere of central Paris.

Evening: Seine Walk

Finish your first day with a relaxed walk beside the Seine. You can stop at a café or restaurant before returning to your hotel.

Day 2: Eiffel Tower, Musée d'Orsay and Saint-Germain

Morning: Eiffel Tower

Start day two with the Eiffel Tower. If you want to go inside or visit one of the viewing levels, check ticket availability before your trip. Arriving earlier in the day can also make the visit easier to manage.

Afternoon: Musée d'Orsay

Continue toward the Musée d'Orsay, known for its impressive collection of European art. Spend as much time as your interests allow before exploring the surrounding area.

Evening: Saint-Germain-des-Prés

Spend the evening around Saint-Germain-des-Prés. This is a good area for cafés, restaurants, shops and a slower evening after a busy sightseeing day.

Day 3: Montmartre, Sacré-Cœur and the Champs-Élysées

Morning: Montmartre

Begin your final day in Montmartre. Explore its streets, small shops, cafés and artistic atmosphere.

Sacré-Cœur

Continue to the Sacré-Cœur area and take some time to enjoy the views across Paris. Wear comfortable shoes because Montmartre includes sloping streets and stairs.

Afternoon: Arc de Triomphe and Champs-Élysées

In the afternoon, head toward the Arc de Triomphe and walk along the Champs-Élysées. This gives you a different view of central Paris and provides an easy way to finish your major sightseeing.

Evening: Final Paris Dinner

For your final evening, choose a restaurant near your hotel or explore another neighbourhood that you enjoyed during the trip.

Where to Stay for 3 Days in Paris

Choosing the right area can make a short Paris trip much easier. Staying near central attractions or a convenient Metro station can reduce travel time.

How to Get Around Paris

Paris is well suited to a combination of walking and public transport. The Metro can save time when attractions are farther apart, while walking is often the best way to experience individual neighbourhoods.

How Much Does 3 Days in Paris Cost?

Your total budget depends heavily on accommodation, restaurants, attractions and transport. A budget-conscious traveller can reduce costs by choosing affordable accommodation and meals, while travellers looking for central hotels and more paid attractions will spend considerably more.

Things to Do in Paris at Night

If you still have energy after sightseeing, explore Paris after dark. Evening walks, cafés and illuminated landmarks can give the city a very different atmosphere.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 3 days enough for Paris?

Three days can provide a good introduction to Paris, especially if you plan your sightseeing by neighbourhood and book important attractions ahead of time.

What should I see first in Paris?

First-time visitors commonly include the Eiffel Tower, Louvre, Notre-Dame area, Montmartre and the Seine in their itinerary.

Should I book Paris attractions in advance?

For popular attractions, checking availability and booking ahead can help you avoid unnecessary waiting and fit more activities into a short trip.

Final Tips for Your Paris Trip

Keep your itinerary flexible. Paris is best experienced at a comfortable pace, so leave some time for unexpected discoveries, cafés and relaxed walks. Comfortable shoes, advance planning and a convenient hotel location can make a significant difference during a three-day visit.
